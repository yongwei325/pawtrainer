Puppies explore the world with their mouths. If your eight-week-old
landshark is treating your hands like chew toys, take a breath — this is
normal, and it is very fixable without a single harsh word.

## Why puppies bite

Biting is how puppies learn bite inhibition from their littermates, and
how they soothe teething discomfort. The goal isn't to *stop* the mouthing
overnight — it's to teach your puppy that human skin is off-limits and that
toys are fair game.

## The force-free fix: redirect, don't punish

1. **Redirect immediately.** The moment teeth touch skin, calmly swap your
   hand for a chew toy. The instant they bite the toy, praise warmly.
2. **Reward the calm.** Catch your puppy chewing appropriate items and mark
   it with a happy "yes!" plus a treat.
3. **Use reverse time-outs.** If biting escalates, stand up, fold your arms,
   and look away for 20–30 seconds. Attention — even "no!" — is rewarding to
   a puppy. Removing it teaches that biting ends the fun.

> Force-free does not mean permissive. It means we teach the behavior we
> want instead of punishing the behavior we don't.

## When to worry

Mouthing should decrease steadily as your puppy matures. If biting is
accompanied by growling, stiff body language, or fear, that is a behavior
question best addressed with a qualified positive-reinforcement trainer.
