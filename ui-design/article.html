How to Stop Puppy Biting at Night (Without Yelling)

JM Jordan Mills, CPDT-KA ·9 min read·Updated Jul 18, 2026

On this page

  1. Why puppies bite more at night
  2. The 3-step redirection routine
  3. 3 mistakes that make it worse
  4. What a normal timeline looks like

If your puppy turns into a shark the moment the lights go down, you're not alone. Night-time mouthing is one of the most common complaints from new dog parents — and the good news is it's completely normal, and very fixable.

This guide skips the old "alpha" myths. Everything below is reward-based and backed by how dogs actually learn. No yelling, no holding the mouth shut, no guilt.

Why puppies bite more at night

Most night biting isn't aggression — it's a cocktail of overtiredness, boredom, and teething discomfort. Puppies under five months explore the world with their mouths, and when they're wound up before bed, your hands become the nearest chew toy.

"A puppy that's biting at 9pm is usually a puppy that needed to be asleep by 8pm." — our trainer team

The 3-step redirection routine

We've used this with hundreds of fosters. It works because it gives the puppy a better option instead of just removing the wrong one.

  1. Spot it early. The second the mouth opens toward skin, say a calm "uh-oh" — no emotion, just information.
  2. Swap, don't scold. Immediately offer a frozen chew or tug toy. The moment teeth touch the toy, mark it: "yes!" and let them enjoy it.
  3. Reset the room. If biting continues, end the play. Stand up, hands folded, eyes away for 30 seconds. Biting ends the fun — consistently.

3 mistakes that make it worse

Most well-meaning owners accidentally reward the biting. Watch for these:

  • Wrestling back. To a puppy, a hand pulling away is a chase game. Keep movements slow and boring.
  • Inconsistent rules. If biting is funny at 6pm but forbidden at 9pm, the lesson never lands.
  • Skipping the nap. An overtired puppy cannot learn. Protect the schedule above all else.

What a normal timeline looks like

For most puppies, intense mouthing peaks around 4 months and fades by 6–7 months as adult teeth come in. You should see clear improvement within 2 weeks of a consistent routine — not perfection, but progress.

If biting is paired with growling over food, freezing, or fear, that's beyond normal mouthing. Reach out to a certified trainer or your vet.
